<h1 class="serif">Kansas Rural Health Transformation Program — Year 1</h1>
<p>Kansas received $221.9 million for the first year of the federal Rural Health Transformation Program — the sixth-largest award in the nation. This tab follows that money from Washington to each named hospital, clinic, agency, vendor, and program: the competitive grants announced in May, the contracts the State signed without competition because they were written into its federal budget request, and the organizations that administer the dollars on the State’s behalf. It also shows what the State has told Washington it has actually committed and paid so far, and which hospitals are forming new networks with this funding.</p>
<div class="last-updated">Updated September 13, 2026 · Year 2 plan + Astra hospital grid completed</div>

<h2 class="serif">$1.1 billion over five years. $221.9 million a year. Here is where it goes.</h2>
<p>In December 2025 the federal government awarded Kansas $221,898,008 a year for five years under the Rural Health Transformation Program. The State of Kansas decides who receives it. This ledger follows every dollar that has been named, announced, obligated, or proposed — and shows what changes from year to year, which hospitals are forming new networks, and who administers the money along the way. It is compiled entirely from public documents.</p>

<details class="card glossary">
<summary>New to this program? Plain-language guide &amp; acronym legend</summary>
<p class="gintro">The Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP) is a federal program created by Congress in July 2025 that sends $50 billion to states over five years (2026–2030) to strengthen rural health care. Kansas received $221.9 million for Year 1. The Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE) runs the state’s program and awards the money to hospitals, clinics, and other organizations through grants and contracts. This tracker follows where every publicly traceable dollar goes.</p>
<div class="ggrid">
<div class="ggroup"><div class="gtitle">The agencies &amp; players</div><dl><dt>CMS</dt><dd>Centers for Medicare &amp; Medicaid Services — the federal agency that awards and oversees the money.</dd><dt>KDHE</dt><dd>Kansas Department of Health and Environment — the state agency running Kansas's program.</dd><dt>SFC</dt><dd>Kansas State Finance Council — the Governor and legislative leaders; receives reports on how federal funds are used.</dd><dt>KHA</dt><dd>Kansas Hospital Association.</dd><dt>KUMC</dt><dd>University of Kansas Medical Center.</dd><dt>KDA</dt><dd>Kansas Dental Association — administers the dental educational awards.</dd></dl></div>
<div class="ggroup"><div class="gtitle">The law &amp; paperwork</div><dl><dt>RHTP</dt><dd>Rural Health Transformation Program — the $50 billion, five-year federal program this tracker follows.</dd><dt>OBBBA</dt><dd>One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1, July 2025) — the federal law that created the program.</dd><dt>SF-424A</dt><dd>The standard federal budget form — shows how the state plans to spend the award, category by category.</dd><dt>RFA</dt><dd>Request for Applications — how the state invites organizations to compete for grant money.</dd></dl></div>
<div class="ggroup"><div class="gtitle">Kansas's grant programs</div><dl><dt>RPGP</dt><dd>Regional Partnerships Grant Program — grants for collaborative projects among rural providers ($49.9M awarded).</dd><dt>REH/CAP</dt><dd>Rural Emergency Hospital Conversion / Transformative Capital Investment Program — building and equipment grants ($31.3M awarded across 18 recipients).</dd><dt>EBP</dt><dd>Evidence-Based Practice Program — payments to hospitals and clinics for adopting proven care practices and reporting quality data.</dd><dt>AHAP</dt><dd>Anchor Hospital Advancement Program — AI and records-system upgrades delivered through large “anchor” hospitals.</dd><dt>CHW + A-FIM</dt><dd>Community Health Worker + Accountable Food Is Medicine — places trained health workers in clinics and funds food-as-medicine services.</dd><dt>PACE</dt><dd>Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly — wraparound medical and social care that helps seniors stay out of nursing homes.</dd><dt>ACO</dt><dd>Accountable Care Organization — a group of providers jointly responsible for the quality and cost of care.</dd><dt>APCD</dt><dd>All Payer Claims Database — a statewide collection of insurance claims used to analyze prices and care patterns.</dd></dl></div>
<div class="ggroup"><div class="gtitle">Types of providers</div><dl><dt>CAH</dt><dd>Critical Access Hospital — a small rural hospital (25 beds or fewer) with special Medicare status.</dd><dt>REH</dt><dd>Rural Emergency Hospital — a federal designation for hospitals that convert to emergency and outpatient care only, with no inpatient beds.</dd><dt>FQHC</dt><dd>Federally Qualified Health Center — a community clinic that must serve patients regardless of their ability to pay.</dd><dt>RHC</dt><dd>Rural Health Clinic — a certified outpatient clinic in a rural underserved area.</dd><dt>CCBHC</dt><dd>Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic — a clinic providing a full range of mental health and addiction services.</dd><dt>CHW</dt><dd>Community Health Worker — a trained local worker who connects residents to care and social services.</dd></dl></div>
<div class="ggroup"><div class="gtitle">How this tracker classifies geography</div><dl><dt>FORHP</dt><dd>Federal Office of Rural Health Policy — sets the federal definition of “rural” used here.</dd><dt>RUCA</dt><dd>Rural-Urban Commuting Area codes — the census-based system behind the urban/rural split.</dd><dt>Frontier</dt><dd>Counties with fewer than 6 people per square mile — the most remote tier.</dd><dt>HQ</dt><dd>Headquarters — classification reflects where the recipient organization is based, not necessarily where it delivers services.</dd></dl></div>
</div>
